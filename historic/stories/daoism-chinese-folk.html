Daoism & Chinese Folk-Religious Landscape

Dao · liturgy · local gods · careful labels

Daoist philosophy and Daoist communal religion are related but not identical; both sit among ancestor reverence, Confucian rite, Buddhist temples, and local deity cults. “Chinese religion” is a landscape, not one steeple.

Shelves: Religions & traditions · Philosophy

Chapter 1 of 4

Classical texts

Daodejing and Zhuangzi inspire quietist and playful critiques of forced order. They become scripture inside later Daoist movements without exhausting Daoism.

Read them also as Warring States political theory.

Chapter 2 of 4

Liturgical Daoism

Celestial Masters, Shangqing, Lingbao, and later schools build priesthoods, registers, and rituals for healing and cosmic renewal.

Immortality arts and inner alchemy occupy specialists — history of practice, not a how-to here.

Chapter 3 of 4

The wider landscape

Village festivals, City God temples, Mazu along coasts, ancestral halls — much everyday sacred life is local and syncretic.

Labels “Daoist” vs “folk” are often scholarly or state categories more than insider ones.

Chapter 4 of 4

Modern pressures

Imperial patronage, Republican science campaigns, Maoist suppression, and reform-era revivals reshaped temples and lineages.

Why it still matters: shows how philosophy, liturgy, and neighborhood gods can share one civilization without merging.
