Shinto

Kami · shrines · not a single founder

Shinto names ways of honoring kami — deities, spirits, and sacred presence in places and lineages — through purification, festival, and shrine practice. It has coexisted, competed, and blended with Buddhism for centuries.

Shelves: Religions & traditions

Chapter 1 of 3

Kami and shrine

Kami range from great mythic figures to local water and mountain presence. Shrines mark thresholds; priests and communities maintain festivals (matsuri).

Purity and pollution structure approach — washing hands as theology in gesture.

Chapter 2 of 3

Buddhism’s long conversation

From the sixth century on, Buddhist temples and Shinto shrines often shared complexes (shinbutsu). Meiji-era separation policies forced clearer institutional lines.

Everyday Japanese practice may visit both without anxiety about exclusive membership.

Chapter 3 of 3

Modern nation and after

State Shinto’s role in imperial ideology before 1945 is historically serious; postwar Shinto continues as shrine networks without that state theology.

Why it still matters: a model of place-based sacrality beside universalizing religions.
