Buddhism

India · Asia · modern world

Siddhartha Gautama’s awakening launches monastic and lay traditions centered on suffering, impermanence, non-self, and liberation. Schools multiply: Theravāda, Mahāyāna, Vajrayāna — with local cultures always co-authoring.

Shelves: Religions & traditions · Philosophy

Chapter 1 of 4

Awakening and early sangha

Dating the Buddha varies; scholarly consensus places him in the earlier centuries BCE. Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path structure early teaching.

Monastic rule (Vinaya) and councils shape community; oral then written canons diverge by school.

Chapter 2 of 4

Spreading roads

Ashokan patronage, Silk Road monks, Sri Lankan chronicles, Chinese pilgrims — Buddhism travels with merchants and kings.

Translation is transformation: Chinese, Tibetan, Southeast Asian Buddhisms are creative, not diluted.

Chapter 3 of 4

Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna

Bodhisattva ideals, emptiness philosophy, Pure Land faith, Chan/Zen paradox, tantric ritual — internal debates as lively as any scholasticism.

Lay devotion and monastic learning intertwine differently by region.

Chapter 4 of 4

Modern encounters

Colonial Orientalsim, nationalist Buddhisms, socially engaged Buddhism, and secular mindfulness extract and reinvent.

Why it still matters: a psychology of craving and release that remains philosophically sharp whether or not one takes refuge.
