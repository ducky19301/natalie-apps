1440 — 1441

A Pope's Debt

Pope Eugene IV owed Florence roughly 25,000 florins. As pledge, he offered the town of Sansepolcro. A stream called the Rio would mark the new border between the Papal States and Florentine territory.

The Mistake

Two Streams, One Name

Both sides agreed the Rio would be the border — but two streams shared that name, about 500 meters apart. The strip between them, including the village of Cospaia, belonged to neither power.

Independence

A Republic by Accident

Locals declared independence. A council of heads of household governed the tiny free zone — tax-free, unarmed, and, by the 1480s, quietly useful as a buffer between greater powers.

1574 onward

The Golden Leaf

Tobacco seeds — the “Tornabuoni herb” — arrived via Alfonso Tornabuoni and Niccolò. Cospaia became an early Italian tobacco center. Papal bans and excommunication elsewhere handed the little republic a monopoly advantage.

Tax-free buffer · Smugglers' haven
The Free Zone

Neither Pope nor Prince

Untaxed and unpoliced, Cospaia thrived as a trading crossroads — and later as a smugglers' haven. For centuries, its peculiar freedom endured precisely because neither neighbor wanted the other to claim it.

1440 — 1826 · nearly four centuries

1826

A Quiet Ending

Fourteen representatives submitted. The republic was divided between the Papal States and Tuscany. Each inhabitant received a silver coin — and permission to keep growing tobacco.
