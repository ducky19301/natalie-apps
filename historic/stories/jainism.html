Jainism

Ahimsa · ascetic extremes · Tirthankaras

Jain tradition centers non-violence (ahimsa), many-sided truth (anekantavada), and liberation through ascetic purification. Mahavira is the most recent Tirthankara in a long lineage — a brief page for a rigorous path.

Tirthankaras and Mahavira

Jains recount twenty-four ford-makers; historically, Parshvanatha and Mahavira (older contemporary of the Buddha in tradition) anchor early history.

Digambara and Śvētāmbara communities diverge on practice and canon.

Chapter 2 of 3

Ahimsa as cosmology

Karma is material sticking to the soul; violence binds. Monks may sweep paths and strain water; lay Jains shaped Indian business ethics of restraint.

Anekantavada’s intellectual humility — multiple viewpoints — is a philosophical gift beyond the community.

Chapter 3 of 3

Why brief still counts

Smaller than Hindu or Buddhist worlds in numbers, Jain influence on Indian thought and nonviolence discourse is outsized.

Why it still matters: ethical maximalism as a lived experiment, not a slogan.
