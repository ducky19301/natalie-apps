Judaism

Torah · covenant · rabbinic tradition · diversity

Judaism is a peoplehood and a religion of covenant, law, and commentary — from ancient Israelite religion through Second Temple variety to rabbinic, medieval, and modern movements. This is overview, not a single synagogue’s creed.

Shelves: Religions & traditions

Chapter 1 of 4

From Israelite religion to Bible

Archaeology and text scholarship describe an evolving monolatry/monotheism among other Levantine cults. Temple, sacrifice, prophecy, and kingship structure ancient practice.

Exile and return reshape identity around Torah and community.

Chapter 2 of 4

Second Temple pluralism

Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, apocalyptic groups, diaspora synagogues — Judaism was already diverse before 70 CE.

The Temple’s destruction pivots energy toward prayer, study, and halakhah.

Chapter 3 of 4

Rabbinic and medieval worlds

Mishnah and Talmuds create a portable homeland of argument. Philosophy (Maimonides), poetry, and Kabbalah flourish under Islamic and Christian rule — with persecution and golden ages both real.

Sephardi and Ashkenazi rites diverge without ceasing to be one people in covenant memory.

Chapter 4 of 4

Modern movements and peoplehood

Emancipation, Reform, Orthodox, Conservative, Reconstructionist, secular Jewish cultures, Zionism, and the Shoah reshape the twentieth century.

Why it still matters: ethical monotheism’s debates, textual arguing as holiness, and minority resilience under empire.

Further reading vibe

Collins on Hebrew Bible intro; Satlow; Boyarin; on mysticism, Scholem carefully; avoid Christian supersessionist frames.
