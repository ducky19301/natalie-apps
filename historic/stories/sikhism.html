Sikhism

Punjab · Gurus · Guru Granth Sahib

Sikh tradition begins with Guru Nanak’s teaching of one Creator, equality, and devotion beyond empty ritual, continuing through ten human Gurus and resting in the scripture as living Guru.

Chapter 1 of 3

Nanak and the Guru lineage

Nanak (1469–1539) teaches naam, honest work, and sharing. Nine successor Gurus develop community institutions under Mughal-era pressures.

The fifth Guru compiles the Adi Granth; the tenth, Gobind Singh, closes the human line and elevates scripture.

Chapter 2 of 3

Khalsa and identity

1699 Khalsa initiation and the Five Ks forge a visible disciplined community. Martial history is self-defense and sovereignty narrative, not mere militarism.

Gurdwara hospitality (langar) enacts equality in the meal line.

Chapter 3 of 3

Modern Sikh worlds

British colonial Punjab, Partition trauma, diaspora gurdwaras, and debates over identity politics shape the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.

Why it still matters: a clear case of a young, scriptural, egalitarian tradition with global reach.
