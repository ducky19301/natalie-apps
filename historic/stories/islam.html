Islam

Qur’an · Prophet · law · cultures

Islam arises in Arabia with the Prophet Muhammad’s revelations, forming a community oriented to one God (Allah), prophetic history shared with Jews and Christians, and a law-ethics complex that spanned continents within a century.

Shelves: Religions & traditions

Chapter 1 of 4

Revelation and community

The Qur’an, in Arabic, is recited as God’s speech. Mecca and Medina periods shape worship, social rules, and conflict. The hijra (622 CE) marks a communal calendar.

Hadith literature later organizes prophetic practice — with sophisticated isnad critique internal to the tradition.

Chapter 2 of 4

Expansion and civilization

Rapid conquests create an empire; conversion of populations is gradual. Arabic becomes a prestige language of science and philosophy.

Sunni and Shia divergences begin in succession disputes and deepen theologically over centuries.

Chapter 3 of 4

Law, mysticism, philosophy

Fiqh schools guide ritual and society; Sufi orders cultivate inward remembrance; falsafa and kalām argue metaphysics.

Andalusian, Persian, Ottoman, West African, and Southeast Asian Islams localize without ceasing to face Mecca.

Chapter 4 of 4

Modern pressures

Colonialism, reformism, nationalism, oil politics, and migration produce competing visions of authenticity.

Why it still matters: nearly two billion people; studying Islam is studying a central thread of world history — respectfully, comparatively, without caricature.
