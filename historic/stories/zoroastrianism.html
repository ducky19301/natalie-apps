Zoroastrianism

Avesta · Asha · Parsi & Iranian Zoroastrians

One of the world’s oldest continuous revealed traditions, associated with the prophet Zarathushtra (Zoroaster), teaches ethical dualism, fire as pure icon, and judgment. Dating and Avestan layers remain scholarly puzzles.

Shelves: Religions & traditions

Chapter 1 of 4

Zarathushtra and the Gathas

The Gathas — archaic Avestan hymns — center Ahura Mazda, asha (truth/order), and human choice. Later Avestan texts expand ritual and cosmogony.

Historical dating estimates vary widely (second millennium to first millennium BCE).

Chapter 2 of 4

Empire and fire

Achaemenid inscriptions invoke Ahuramazda; Sasanian Iran makes Zoroastrian institutions central. Fire temples and purity laws structure community life.

Good thoughts, words, deeds remain ethical shorthand.

Chapter 3 of 4

Afterlife and cosmic drama

Soul judgment, heaven/hell imagery, and final renovation (Frashokereti) mark a distinctive eschatology that later traditions may echo — scholars debate pathways of influence.

Dualism of Spenta Mainyu and Angra Mainyu is ethical-cosmic, not identical to later Christian Satan myths.

Chapter 4 of 4

Living minorities

Parsi communities in India and Zoroastrians in Iran and diaspora maintain identity under demographic pressure. Modern reform and orthodoxy debates continue.

Why it still matters: a small community carries a large historical vocabulary of ethics and cosmic hope.
