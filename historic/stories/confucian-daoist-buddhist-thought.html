Confucian, Daoist & Buddhist Thought

Overview · self-cultivation · emptiness · governance

Three large streams — sometimes rivals, sometimes blended — shaped East Asian ethics, metaphysics, and statecraft. This is a doorway essay, not a substitute for their canons.

Shelves: Philosophy · Religions & traditions

Chapter 1 of 4

Confucian repair of ritual personhood

Kongzi (Confucius) and later Mencius/Xunzi argue about human nature, ren (humaneness), and li (ritual propriety) as trainable. Government begins in rectified relationships.

Imperial exams later institutionalize Confucian texts — power marrying pedagogy.

Chapter 2 of 4

Daoist ways and unways

Daodejing and Zhuangzi praise soft power, uselessness, and skepticism toward forced naming. Daoist communities also build liturgies, alchemy, and local god cults — philosophy and religion intertwined.

“Going with the Dao” is political critique as much as spa slogan.

Chapter 3 of 4

Buddhist arrivals and transformations

From Indic origins, Buddhist schools enter China via Central Asia, translating emptiness, karma, and monastic discipline into new idioms (Tiantai, Chan/Zen, Pure Land).

Korean and Japanese receptions further remix. Conflict and coexistence with Confucian elites recur.

Chapter 4 of 4

Reading them together

Neo-Confucians absorb and answer Buddhist metaphysics; modern reformers pick and choose. Avoid treating “Eastern thought” as one calm lake.

Why it still matters: these vocabularies still structure education, protest, and mindfulness industries worldwide.
