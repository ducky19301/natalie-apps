Christianity — Origins & Diversity

Not only Western · many churches

Christianity begins among Jewish followers of Jesus in the Roman East and becomes a family of churches spanning Coptic, Syriac, Greek, Latin, Ethiopian, Armenian, Protestant, Pentecostal, and more. Western Latin stories are one branch, not the trunk alone.

Shelves: Religions & traditions

Chapter 1 of 4

Jesus movement in Jewish context

Jesus of Nazareth preached in Galilee and Judea under Roman rule; crucifixion and reports of resurrection launch a messianic movement still debating Torah, gentiles, and temple.

Paul’s letters — earliest Christian documents — argue inclusion of gentiles. Gospels interpret Jesus for later communities.

Chapter 2 of 4

Empire, creed, and split paths

From persecuted minority to Constantinian favor, councils define Trinity and Christology — while “heresy” labels losers of imperial-theological contests.

Persian, Armenian, Ethiopian, and Indian Christianities grow on other roads; Chalcedonian divides shape East/West.

Chapter 3 of 4

Medieval to reformations

Monasticism, scholasticism, Orthodox liturgy, and Latin papacy organize different sacral politics. Protestant reformations pluralize Europe; Catholic reform answers.

Mission and empire entangle from the Iberian age onward — conversion often under coercion, sometimes under local agency.

Chapter 4 of 4

Global majority Christianity

Today’s demographic center of gravity is in Africa, Latin America, and parts of Asia. Pentecostal and independent churches innovate vigorously.

Why it still matters: studying only “Western Christianity” misreads both past and present.

Diversity is original, not a modern afterthought.
