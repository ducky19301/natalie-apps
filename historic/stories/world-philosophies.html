World Philosophies — A Hub Essay

Greek · Indian darśanas · Chinese · Islamic golden age

Philosophy did not happen only in Athens. This hub sketches several classical families of argument — not to flatten them into one “wisdom tradition,” but to invite cross-reading without a European finish line.

Shelves: Philosophy · Thinkers

Chapter 1 of 5

Greek habits of definition

From Pre-Socratics through Hellenistic schools (Stoic, Epicurean, Skeptic), Greek philosophy prizes public argument, logic, and ethics under civic pressure.

Its prestige in later Europe is historical fact — not proof of monopoly.

Chapter 2 of 5

Indian darśanas (overview)

Classical “viewpoints” include Nyāya (logic), Vaiśeṣika (categories), Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta, plus Buddhist and Jain systems often counted alongside. Debate culture (vāda) is rigorous and technical.

Sanskrit and Prakrit texts treat knowledge, self, liberation, and language with centuries of commentary — peer to any Mediterranean archive.

Chapter 3 of 5

Chinese argument and self-cultivation

Confucian, Daoist, Mohist, and Legalist teachings contest ritual, government, and the Way. Later Neo-Confucian synthesis meets Buddhist challenge.

Philosophy here is often inseparable from governance and moral psychology — a feature, not a lack of “pure reason.”

Chapter 4 of 5

Islamic golden age philosophy

Falsafa engages Aristotle and Plato through Arabic; kalām develops theology-as-argument; figures like Ibn Sīnā, Ibn Rushd, and al-Fārābī travel into Latin Europe.

Persian and Andalusian contexts matter; so do critiques of falsafa from within Islamic thought.

Comparison without ranking is a discipline.
